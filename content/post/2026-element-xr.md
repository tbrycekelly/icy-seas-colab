Laura Whitmore and colleagues at the University of Alaska Fairbanks have received a $877,501 award through the National Science Foundation's Major Research Instrumentation program to establish a new high-resolution inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry facility at the International Arctic Research Center. The facility's Element XR will be the only instrument of its kind in Alaska, bringing parts-per-trillion elemental analysis closer to the researchers, students, agencies, and industries working across the state.

The instrument will support precise measurements of elements and isotope ratios at very low concentrations. Its applications span marine and sea-ice biogeochemistry, water quality, environmental contaminants, critical minerals, paleoceanography, carbon cycling, and research on changes such as Alaska's rusting rivers. Just as importantly, the facility will reduce the need to send samples out of state and strengthen continuity for environmental analyses as older high-resolution instruments around the country reach the end of their operational lives.

Whitmore leads the award with Ana Aguilar-Islas of the UAF College of Fisheries and Ocean Sciences and Mette Kaufman of the International Arctic Research Center. The team plans to open the facility in stages—first to the university research community, then more broadly—with public access planned by the end of 2029. Education is part of the facility from the outset, including analytical chemistry instruction, graduate researcher training, and summer opportunities for rural students.

## Supporting the laboratory around the instrument

A major scientific instrument reaches its potential when the systems around it are dependable, transparent, and usable by a broad community. Icy Seas will help explore that surrounding infrastructure through a smart-laboratory pilot program developed with the UAF team.

The pilot will focus on three connected areas:

- **Smart laboratory infrastructure:** practical systems for instrument status, environmental monitoring, sample and workflow tracking, and clear operational handoffs. The goal is to make routine work visible and auditable without adding unnecessary overhead.
- **Data management:** durable structures for sample metadata, quality-control records, analytical outputs, processing history, and release-ready data packages. Raw observations and derived products should remain linked through explicit provenance.
- **Analytical capacity building:** documentation, reproducible workflows, training materials, and hands-on support that help students and collaborators move confidently from sample preparation through analysis and interpretation.

This is a pilot rather than a finished facility platform. It is an opportunity to learn alongside the instrument team, identify the highest-value points for automation and standardization, and build only what improves scientific reliability and access. The long-term aim is straightforward: pair exceptional analytical capability with laboratory systems that make the resulting science easier to trust, reuse, and sustain.
